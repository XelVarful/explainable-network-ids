# 01 — Mohale and Obagbuwa (2025)

**Paper:** Evaluating machine learning-based intrusion detection systems with explainable AI: enhancing transparency and interpretability  
**Authors:** Vincent Zibi Mohale and Ibidun Christiana Obagbuwa  
**Journal:** Frontiers in Computer Science, 2025  
**Source:** https://doi.org/10.3389/fcomp.2025.1520741

## Research objective

Compare models for network intrusion detection and identify which traffic
features influence their predictions.

## Dataset and preprocessing

The authors used UNSW-NB15. Section 3.1 describes feature selection using
recursive feature elimination and correlation analysis, Min-Max scaling for
numerical features, categorical encoding, and SMOTE for class imbalance.

## Reported results

| Model | Accuracy | F1 | ROC-AUC |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Decision Tree | 87% | 0.86 | 0.92 |
| MLP | 85.98% | 0.85 | 0.91 |
| XGBoost | 86.87% | 0.86 | 0.93 |
| Gaussian Naive Bayes | 73% | 0.72 | 0.83 |
| CatBoost | 87% | 0.87 | 0.94 |

Source: Section 4.2, Table 4. CatBoost and Decision Tree tied on reported
accuracy. CatBoost had the highest reported F1 and ROC-AUC.

## SHAP findings

For XGBoost, `ct_dst_sport_ltm` had the largest average impact on the
model output. Source-to-destination time to live (`sttl`) was also influential.

## Limitations and relevance to our study

Table 4 gives one F1 value per model without specifying the averaging
method in the table. We therefore record it as reported F1. A comparison
with our planned macro-F1 would require matching metric definitions.

Our study can test whether the same features remain important across models
and whether they help explain errors on specific attack types.
